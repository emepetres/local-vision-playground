# Voice: currently Javier Carnero's

The register the deck's prose follows. **Swappable**: another speaker adapts the skill by editing this one file. It holds Javier's voice, derived from his written prose in `emepetres/articulos` (`CONTEXT.md` "Voz", `docs/tics-de-voz.md`).

## What voice governs

Voice **owns register** over slide text, `::: notes`, `::: script` and the thesis as it appears on a slide. It leaves out quotations (`quote` slides carry the source's words), code, image placeholders and the agent's own chat.

Two registers:

- **Slide text**: fragments, no paragraphs. A slide line reads at a glance.
- **Spoken** (`::: script`, `::: notes`): sentences of a **median 10–14 words**, shorter than the written source this voice was measured on, because the room hears each sentence once. In Spanish or English alike.

## Register

Restrained and precise. Measured against ~3,770 words of source prose:

- **No emoji.** Ever.
- **At most one exclamation mark** in a whole talk.
- **Sparse em dashes**: roughly one per talk.
- **Few hedges**: "maybe", "probably", "I think" appear about four times in 3,770 words. State the claim.
- Plain nouns over jargon. A concrete cost over an abstraction.

## Argument moves

Javier's prose tends to move like this. They are **suggestions** the outline may take or leave, never rules the deck enforces:

- **Open on a credited personal fact**: a specific thing the speaker did or saw, owned and dated.
- **Build to the claim**: the thesis is earned across the body, not announced up front.
- **Run on a mistake with a named cost**: the argument turns on a concrete error and what it cost.
- **Close by denying the small reading and asserting the large one.**

## Visual register

Image placeholders stay concrete and explanatory: a diagram or a metaphor that carries the claim, never decoration.

## Swapping this file

Replace the register, the bans and the argument moves with the new speaker's. Keep the section structure.
